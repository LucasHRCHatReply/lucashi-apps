A tidy email signature tells people in a few lines who you are, how to reach you and where to find you. This tool builds one from your details: name, job title, company, phone, website, social links, photo or logo, and colour. Four templates are available: **Classic** (photo on the left, coloured rule), **Modern** (large name, contacts under a line), **Plain** (almost only text) and **Banner** (contact details in white on the colour you choose).

## How to

1. Fill in the fields. The preview updates as you type, and empty fields disappear from the signature.
2. Pick a template and a colour, and add a photo or logo if you like.
3. Click Copy signature, open your mail app's settings and paste.

## Where to paste it

**Gmail.** Gear icon, See all settings, Signature section, Create new. Paste into the box with Cmd+V (Ctrl+V on Windows), choose it for new emails and replies, then save at the bottom of the page.

**Outlook (web).** Settings, Accounts, Signatures, New signature. Paste into the editor and set your default signature.

**Outlook (Mac).** Outlook, Settings, Signatures, +. Same idea. If the layout looks squeezed, save it and reopen it: Outlook then rebuilds the table.

**Apple Mail.** Mail, Settings, Signatures, +. Untick "Always match my default message font" before pasting, otherwise Mail replaces the signature's font.

The Download signature.html button gives you a file to open in a browser: select the signature there and copy it. It is handy as a backup, or to pass to a colleague.

## Why tables and not modern code

Mail apps do not display web pages the way a browser does. Gmail strips style sheets, and Outlook on Windows relies on the Word rendering engine. A dependable signature therefore uses tables, styles written directly on each element, system fonts (the ones already on the device) and explicit dimensions. That is exactly what this tool produces: no style sheet, no class, no script, and a width capped at 600 pixels.

Social networks appear as text links (LinkedIn, Instagram and so on) rather than icons: text is never blocked, depends on no hosting and stays readable when a mail app hides images.

## The photo or logo

You can drop an image: it is reduced to 240 pixels on this page and never leaves your device. But an image embedded straight into the text is frequently blocked by Gmail and Outlook on the recipient's side, or turned into an attachment. For a dependable result, host the image at an https address you control and paste that address in Image address. The tool reminds you as soon as an image is embedded.

## Light and dark preview

The Dark button simulates what mail apps do in dark mode, where light colours are inverted. If your signature stays readable in both previews, it will be readable almost everywhere. Avoid very pale colours, which vanish on a white background.
